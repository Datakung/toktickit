## My Reflection

My prompts became more effective when I stated the current Issue boundary, existing behavior that must not regress, and the exact evidence needed before completion. A broad first decomposition hid overlapping Attachment scope and an incomplete release gate, so I requested a critical audit and rejected the original boundaries; during Issue #12 I also corrected the drafted category ordering instead of blindly changing persisted behavior. Peer review and fresh-database tests exposed navigation, history, numeric-boundary, fixture-cleanup, and database-target isolation details that the initial prompts missed, which I reproduced and converted into explicit guards and regressions rather than treating AI output or an initial green run as proof of correctness.
